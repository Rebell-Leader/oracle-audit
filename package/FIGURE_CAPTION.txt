Two families of in-silico AMP oracles disagree in sign on the central validity contrast: (a) at the conventional p>=0.5 threshold the three widely-used published classifiers flag 87.5-87.6% of composition-preserving shuffled decoys and 77.8-79.8% of curated non-AMPs but only 65.7-66.8% of real curated AMPs (AMPredictor flags >99% of everything), inverting the ordering that OmegAMP's hard-negative-trained broad classifier gets right (14.3% / 0.0% / 0.3%); (b) screening real AMPs out of a 4,000-sequence 50/50 pool against those decoys, every oracle looks near-perfect at the published precision@100 depth, but the family-B oracles fall below 0.75 precision at k=240-265 (AMPredictor at k=1) versus k=691 for OmegAMP's broad classifier — so a pipeline selecting tens of thousands of candidates is operating where these oracles are near chance.
